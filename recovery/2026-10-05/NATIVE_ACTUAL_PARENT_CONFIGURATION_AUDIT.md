## One population-rich parent

The point core K is unpacked once. Its literal edge cut has W=mass K(pointWeight E2). Actual mixed-label cleanup constructs H with W≤2|H| and all mixed fibers dense before p is chosen. Native global shading density and the actual F1/G selections then give one parent p with

`mu * |R_p| ≤ delta * |H_p|`,

where `mu=lambda*W*delta^eta/(2*F1*G*|E2|)`. The checked per-row upper gives retention `mu/(16*volumeConstant)` against EVERY same-R reference subfamily, including E1 and E2. This corrects the fact that retention against original incidence mass alone cannot force full-R tube population.

The same p carries the exact mixed raw-grain lower. E1 conditional lower/upper are retained. E2 lower is paid through its literal E1-parent retention, its upper is derived from the original E1 hereditary theorem, and its anisotropic comparison is derived from its installed short-row fields. Cleaned H gets a paid conditional lower and an E1-derived upper. No native admission, multiplicity, population, count, profile or grain-density output certificate is a new input to the source theorem.

## Remaining composition

The source stores p and its population inequality. Explicit descendant phase/height readbacks must be derived from THAT p, rather than invoking a second parent selector. Height alignment, fixed graph coordinates, residue selection and metric slope control are separate proved building blocks; they are not yet claimed as fields of this endpoint. Their whole-node cuts preserve each surviving mixed fiber, with fixed retention costs paid explicitly.

The auxiliary original-incidence selector is valid only for incidence retention; it is not used as a population-density bridge.
